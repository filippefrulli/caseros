# Finding the First Sellers for Caseros

A go-to-market guide for recruiting the first sellers onto caseros — a European-owned
marketplace for handmade & homemade goods (an EU-focused Etsy alternative: individual +
trader sellers, EU-only shipping, 5% commission, Stripe Connect payouts).

---

## 0. Read this first: the one thing that matters

Caseros is a two-sided marketplace, so it has the classic chicken-and-egg problem: buyers
won't come without sellers, sellers won't stay without buyers. **The entire early game is
solving the supply side (sellers) by hand, in a tiny geographic + category niche, and
faking demand until it's real.**

Almost every successful marketplace did unscalable, founder-driven seller recruiting at the
start:

- **Airbnb** founders went door-to-door in NYC photographing listings themselves.
- **Faire** (wholesale marketplace) hand-recruited the first makers one by one and
  personally guaranteed retailers' orders.
- **The Artisans Cooperative** — the closest analog — was born out of the 2022 Etsy seller
  strikes, recruited disgruntled Etsy sellers directly, and grew as a seller-owned co-op.
  These people are *actively looking to leave Etsy right now.*
- **Etsy itself** started by recruiting from existing craft forums and Brooklyn craft fairs.

The goal for the next 60–90 days is **not** "launch to the public." It's to get **10–30 real
sellers actively listing and getting at least one sale each.** That's it.

---

## 1. The unfair advantage: the "Buy European" tailwind

Most marketplace founders have a generic value prop ("lower fees!"). Caseros has a *movement*
behind it. The `/r/BuyFromEU` energy — Europeans deliberately shifting spend away from US
platforms toward European-owned alternatives — is the single best asset. Lean into it hard.

**Positioning to sellers:**

> "A European-owned marketplace for handmade & homemade goods. Your money, your data, and
> your customers stay in Europe. Sellers ship within the EU — no customs nightmares, no
> 3-week transatlantic shipping, lower fees than Etsy, and you're not funding a US tech giant."

Concrete hooks that are built into the product:

- **EU-only shipping by default** (AT, BE, DE, ES, FR, IE, IT, NL, PT) → "no customs charges,
  fast intra-EU delivery."
- **5% commission** → far cheaper than Etsy's effective ~10–15%+ once you add listing fees,
  payment fees, mandatory ads, and offsite ads fees. *Lead with this number.*
- **European-owned / GDPR-native / data stays in the EU.**

This positioning is the recruiting magnet. It's why the first Reddit lead said yes before the
product even existed.

---

## 2. Define the "atomic network" (don't recruit randomly)

Andrew Chen's *Cold Start Problem* core idea: find the **smallest network that can stand on
its own.** If 30 sellers are scattered across 30 countries and 30 categories, no buyer ever
sees a full, browsable selection — it feels empty and everyone churns.

**Pick ONE wedge and saturate it.** Two ways to slice caseros:

| Slice by category | Slice by country |
|---|---|
| e.g. **handmade ceramics & home goods** | e.g. **Germany + Netherlands + Belgium** (high purchasing power, strong "buy local/EU" sentiment, good logistics) |

Recommendation: **pick one category vertical first** (ceramics, candles & soaps, jewelry,
knitwear, or printables/art — whichever the first Reddit lead and her network are in). A buyer
landing on caseros should think "oh, this is *the* place for European handmade ceramics," not
"this is a sparse general store."

### Ideal first seller (ICP)

- Already sells handmade goods on **Etsy** (proven they can produce, photograph, ship, do
  customer service — Etsy did the hard vetting for you).
- **Frustrated with Etsy** (rising fees, forced ads, mass-produced/dropshipped competition
  diluting "handmade," US-platform fatigue).
- Based in the EU and already shipping within the EU.
- Small enough to have time to talk to you, big enough to take their shop seriously.

---

## 3. Turn the Reddit lead into the first "design partner"

This person is worth 50x a random signup. Don't treat her as user #1 — treat her as a
**co-founder of the supply side.**

1. **Get on a call** (video or voice). Not just chat. 30 minutes.
2. **Ask, don't pitch.** Learn: What does she hate about Etsy? What would make her *actually
   move* vs. just add a second channel? What's her #1 fear about a new platform (probably:
   "will I get any sales?")? How does she currently get traffic?
3. **Onboard her shop yourself, by hand.** Offer to migrate her listings for her — pull her
   Etsy listings, images, descriptions, and set up her caseros shop so she has to do almost
   nothing. This "concierge onboarding" is the single highest-leverage thing you can do.
4. **Give her founder status:** lifetime 0% or reduced commission, "Founding Seller" badge, a
   say in the roadmap. Early sellers who feel ownership become evangelists.
5. **Ask for intros.** "Who are 3 people in your maker community who feel the same way about
   Etsy?" Warm intros convert 10x better than cold outreach. This is how you get sellers 2–10
   without doing cold outreach at all.

She is the **referral flywheel ignition.** Most of the first 20 sellers should come through her
and the people she introduces.

---

## 4. Where to find the first 10–50 sellers

Ranked by likely conversion. Spend 80% of your time on the top three.

### Tier 1 — warm, high-intent (start here)

1. **The Reddit lead's referrals** (above). Highest conversion.
2. **`/r/BuyFromEU` and related subs** — `/r/Etsy`, `/r/EtsySellers`,
   `/r/CraftFairsAndMarkets`, `/r/somethingimade`, country subs (`/r/de`, `/r/Netherlands`),
   and craft-business subs. *Read the rules — most ban overt self-promo.* The right move is to
   **participate genuinely**, comment helpfully, and when sellers complain about Etsy fees
   (constant), reply or DM with "I'm building exactly this, would love your feedback." You're
   recruiting, not advertising.
3. **Etsy seller exodus communities** — Etsy seller Facebook groups, the Artisans Cooperative
   community, Etsy strike/boycott forums. These people have *already decided to leave.* You're
   giving them a destination.

### Tier 2 — where EU makers already gather

4. **Instagram & TikTok hashtags** — `#handmadeineurope`, `#buropean`,
   `#supportsmallbusiness`, `#handmadeingermany`, `#keramik`, `#madeinitaly`, plus
   country/craft combos. DM sellers individually with a personal note (reference their actual
   work). Tedious but high-converting.
5. **Local craft fairs & maker markets** (Weihnachtsmärkte, design markets, makers' markets).
   The Airbnb/Etsy move: show up in person, talk to makers at their stalls, hand them a card.
   Highest trust, doesn't scale — perfect for the first 20.
6. **Pinterest, and existing maker marketplaces** — browse sellers and reach out directly.

### Tier 3 — broader nets (later)

7. **Maker/craft newsletters, blogs, podcasts, and craft-supply shops** (offline + online) —
   partnerships where they tell their audience about caseros.
8. **University art/design departments & craft guilds/cooperatives** — guilds give you a
   cluster of vetted makers at once.
9. **Indie Hacker / founder communities** for feedback, not sellers.

## 5. The outreach playbook (what to actually say)

Cold DM template (Instagram/Reddit) — short, specific, human, leads with *their* benefit:

> Hi [name] — your [specific product, e.g. "speckled stoneware mugs"] are gorgeous. I'm
> building **caseros**, a European-owned alternative to Etsy for handmade goods — 5%
> commission instead of Etsy's ~13%+, EU-only shipping so no customs hassle, and your data
> stays in Europe. I'm hand-picking a small group of founding sellers and would love to set up
> your shop for you (free, I'll migrate your listings). No catch — I just need great makers to
> launch with. Open to a quick chat?

Rules that make this work:

- **Reference their actual work** in the first line. Generic = ignored.
- **Lead with the concrete numbers** (5% vs ~13%, EU shipping).
- **Remove all friction** — "I'll set up your shop for you" beats "sign up here."
- **Scarcity is honest here** — "founding sellers," "hand-picking a small group." True, and it
  works.
- **Ask for a conversation, not a signup.**

---

## 6. The seller value proposition (memorize this)

When a seller asks "why should I bother?", there are five answers:

1. **Cheaper** — 5% commission, no listing fees, no forced ads. ("On a €40 sale you keep ~€38
   vs ~€34 on Etsy.")
2. **European & ethical** — buyers are actively seeking EU-made; caseros puts them in front of
   that demand.
3. **Real handmade** — curated, no mass-produced/dropshipped junk diluting the category (a top
   Etsy-seller grievance).
4. **No customs / fast shipping** — EU-only logistics.
5. **You matter here** — founding seller status, direct line to the founder, influence over the
   roadmap, not seller #4,000,000.

Be honest about the one weakness — **"we're new, traffic is small"** — and counter it: founder
pricing, you're driving the first buyers yourself, and being early means being the top result
in their category when buyers arrive.

---

## 7. Solve demand so sellers don't churn (the other half)

Sellers leave if they get zero sales. You can't recruit your way out of that. In parallel with
seller recruiting:

- **Be the first traffic source yourself.** Drive buyers via the same `/r/BuyFromEU` / "buy
  European" audiences, an Instagram/TikTok account showcasing sellers' products, and a simple
  email list. Content marketing ("10 European ceramicists to buy from instead of Amazon")
  doubles as buyer acquisition *and* seller flattery (sellers reshare posts featuring them →
  free reach).
- **Manufacture the first transactions.** Get friends, family, and yourself to make real
  purchases so every founding seller gets an early sale and a review. "Money moves people" — a
  seller with 3 sales and 2 five-star reviews stays; a seller with 0 leaves. Keep incentives
  **temporary and tied to real activity.**
- **Seed reviews and social proof** so the marketplace doesn't feel empty.
- **Concentrate demand** on the atomic network (one category/region) so limited buyer traffic
  actually converts instead of spreading thin.

---

## 8. Incentives to offer founding sellers

- **0% or reduced commission for life** (or first 12 months) for the first N sellers.
- **"Founding Seller" badge** + featured placement on the homepage and category pages.
- **Free concierge onboarding** (you migrate their listings).
- **Co-marketing** — feature them on caseros' social channels.
- **Direct roadmap influence** — a private group chat with the founder.

Keep the *transaction* incentives temporary and activity-based; make the *status* incentives
permanent (status is cheap and creates loyalty).

---

## 9. What "working" looks like (metrics)

Don't track signups. Track **activated supply + real transactions:**

- # of sellers who have **published ≥3 listings** (activated, not just registered).
- # of sellers with **≥1 real sale.**
- # of **completed transactions / week** and whether it's growing.
- **Seller referrals** — are sellers bringing other sellers? (The flywheel signal.)
- Qualitatively: would the founding sellers be **upset if caseros disappeared?** That's supply-
  side product-market fit.

First-phase target: **~10–20 activated sellers in one category, each with at least one sale,
plus 2+ sellers acquired purely through referral.** Then expand to the next category/region.

---

## 10. Pitfalls to avoid

- **Going wide too early.** 200 sellers across all of Europe and every category = an empty-
  feeling store. Saturate one slice first.
- **Recruiting sellers but ignoring demand.** Sellers with no sales churn and bad-mouth you.
  Demand is the real bottleneck.
- **Auto-blasting communities with spammy promo.** You'll get banned from exactly the
  subreddits/groups you need. Participate first, recruit second.
- **Making sellers do the work.** Every step offloaded onto them (manual listing entry, photo
  uploads) loses sellers. Do it for them at the start.
- **Optimizing the product instead of talking to sellers.** The developer's trap. The first 20
  sellers come from conversations, not code.

## 11. The concrete 30-day plan

**Week 1 — Anchor seller**
- Call the Reddit lead. Hand-migrate her shop. Give her founding status. Get 3 referral intros.
- Decide the **one category + region wedge** based on her niche.

**Week 2 — Referrals + warm communities**
- Reach out to her 3 referrals + start genuine participation in `/r/EtsySellers`,
  `/r/BuyFromEU`, and 2–3 Etsy-exodus Facebook groups. Goal: 5 conversations.
- Stand up a caseros Instagram/TikTok and post the first founding seller's products.

**Week 3 — Direct outreach + first sales**
- 20–30 personalized DMs to handmade sellers in the category (reference their work). Offer
  concierge onboarding.
- Drive/manufacture the **first real sales** so early sellers get a transaction + review.

**Week 4 — Tighten the loop**
- Get to **10 activated sellers** (≥3 listings each).
- Write one "buy European handmade [category]" content piece featuring the sellers; they
  reshare → buyer traffic.
- Ask every happy seller: "Who else should I talk to?" → restart the flywheel.

---

## The whole strategy in one sentence

Use the "Buy European" movement and the Reddit lead to hand-recruit 10–20 frustrated Etsy
sellers in a *single category*, set up their shops for them, manufacture their first sales so
they stay, and let their referrals + happy testimonials pull in the next wave.
